Navigation that holds when a sensor doesn’t.

Research

Integrated navigation is mostly a study of failure modes. GNSS drops out under a bridge, inertial solutions drift without bound, LiDAR needs structure, cameras need light, and radar sees through weather but returns a sparse, noisy cloud. My work is on combining them so the estimate holds where any one of them would not.

Timing and sensor rigs

Disciplining the microcontroller clock to GNSS and triggering and timestamping each sensor in hardware, so every measurement reaching the filter sits on one timeline.

Radar–visual ego-motion

A cascaded estimator that fuses FMCW radar Doppler velocity with monocular visual odometry into one scaled velocity aid for an error-state Kalman filter. The radar, not the IMU, fixes the visual scale, so the visual channel stays statistically independent of the inertial one.

Map-aided positioning

A smaller line of work: testing HD maps as an absolute reference, from matching visual point clouds to HD vector maps (a feasibility study in the dissertation) to LiDAR scan matching against HD point-cloud maps.
